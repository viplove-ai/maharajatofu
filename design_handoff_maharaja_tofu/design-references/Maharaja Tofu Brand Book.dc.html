02
RECOMMENDATION — DIRECTION A, ENAMEL BOARD

The enamel board is the only one of the three that is royal and everyday in the same gesture. A painted indigo signboard with cream lettering is what a jeweller uses and what a tyre-repair shop uses; grandeur is in the craft of the lettering, not in the price of the ink. That is exactly the register “Maharaja Tofu” needs — generous, Indian, confident, and completely at home in a Vasundhara kitchen.

It also wins on the constraints. Two flat colours plus a knockout is the native state of the system, not a compromise version of it; the wordmark holds at 8 mm on a lid, and no edge depends on registration. Deep indigo photographs as a solid dark field under warm fridge light where cream, marigold and vermilion stay bright — the pack stands out beside Mother Dairy's light blue-and-white rather than competing with it.

We take Direction B's best move with us: the ingredient list becomes the hero panel, set in mono at 20 pt with the batch code and packing date treated as typography. Transparency stays a design feature; the identity stops being a paper-and-stamp boutique.

WHY NOT THE OTHER TWO
B, Bahi-Khata — best story, weakest shelf. Off-white and thin rules go grey in a fridge photo, and “handmade ledger” reads expensive to a customer comparing against a ₹90 paneer packet.
C, Miniature/Grid — most visible, least trustworthy. Four equal saturated fields leave the three-ingredient list nowhere to be loud, and cheap sticker printing will show colour gaps on every block edge.
DECISION → A, with B's ingredient-panel typography absorbed. Build out below.

THREE-METRE FRIDGE TEST
The only cue that has to work at distance is the cap band: a full 40 mm ring of marigold or chilli, uninterrupted, top and bottom of the lid. Wordmark, ingredient panel and claim are all near-field information.
Against a Mother Dairy paneer packet the indigo body reads as the darkest object on the shelf — which is what makes a 200 g tub findable in a crowded home fridge.
PRINT SPEC — 2 spot colours (indigo + SKU) on white PP wrap, black overprint for dates and batch. Ingredient panel is a knockout, no tints. Minimum type 7 pt. No bleed-critical alignment: every panel edge tolerates 1.5 mm shift.

09
ONE-PAGE BRAND RULEBOOK
VOICE
HINGLISH HITS,
ENGLISH EXPLAINS
Headlines in Hinglish, short, spoken, never translated word-for-word. Body copy in plain English with the arithmetic shown. Numbers always sourced and always exact.
TONE — the aunty next door who happens to be precise. Warm, specific, unbothered. No wellness vocabulary: no “clean eating”, no “guilt-free”, no “superfood”.
ALWAYS SAY
1. “Teen cheezein. Bas.”
Three ingredients, printed large, every single time.
2. “Aaj subah bana.”
The made-on date and batch number, on the pack and in the post.
3. “Kitchen aa jao.”
The open invitation to visit — a standing offer, not a campaign.
APPROVED CLAIM SET — same protein per serving · half the calories · zero cholesterol · half the saturated fat · lactose-free · swaps into any paneer recipe 1:1.
NEVER SAY
1. Never claim more protein than paneer.
Per 100 g, paneer has more. Our claim is per serving, and it is exact: 180 g tofu = 100 g paneer = 18 g protein. Customers check.
2. Never attack another brand or a local paneer seller.
No adulteration statistics, no “real vs fake”, no side-by-side. We publish our own list and stop talking.
3. Never sell it as giving up paneer.
No diet framing, no “cheat day”, no before-and-after bodies. It is a swap, not a sacrifice.
PHOTOGRAPHY — MAKING TOFU LOOK APPETISING, NOT PALE
DO
Hard side light, 45° — tofu only reads as solid when one edge is bright and the cut face is in shadow. Always show a cut or torn face; never a whole smooth block. Char, tawa marks, mustard-oil sheen. Steel plates, granite, a marble chakla — Indian surfaces, never a white board. Hands in frame. Shoot at 10 cm, food warm, oil still moving.
DON'T
No flat frontal flash and no white-on-white — that is where tofu turns grey. No cold white balance; hold 4300–4800 K so the cream reads warm. No microgreens, no chia, no wooden bowls of raw soybeans. No cling-film shine. Never retouch the cubes into perfect geometry.
